# Benchmark Guidance

The paper proposes benchmark design that keeps protocol compliance separate from the normative content of the deployment policy. Each scenario can retain four annotation layers: evidential status, candidate stakeholder claims, an explicit standing profile `S_{Π,d}`, and any applicable resolution warrant.

When standing is contested, several declared policy profiles can be retained for the same scenario. The same underlying case may then yield different permitted regimes under different policies while prevalence invariance, warrant recording, and the other protocol properties remain testable conditional on each policy.

Matched multilingual and geographically imbalanced retrieval variants can further test whether a standing-grounded perspective disappears because it is less retrievable or because the declared policy excludes it. Sparse representation may reflect publication access, language coverage, or institutional visibility.

This benchmark structure supports technical evaluation of implementation fidelity alongside governance analysis of the policies that determine standing and authority.
