# Pluralism Gate Terminology

This glossary collects the principal terms used in the paper in the sense in which the framework defines them.

## Representation prevalence

Representation prevalence describes how often a position appears in retrieved, elicited, or training material. The framework treats prevalence as a property of the information environment, separate from stakeholder standing and decision authority.

## Stakeholder standing

Stakeholder standing identifies which stakeholders have a recognized basis for bearing on a proposition or decision under the applicable deployment policy. The paper allows standing to arise from affectedness, jurisdiction or delegated authority, epistemic position, rights or entitlement, or another domain-specific rule.

## Decision authority

Decision authority concerns whether the applicable policy authorizes an agent to commit to one contested outcome while material standing-grounded conflict remains.

## Standing relation

`S_{Π,d}` is the policy-supplied relation between stakeholders and action-relevant propositions for which they have a recognized basis for bearing on the decision.

## Admissible reason

An admissible reason is a material reason that survives the applicable evidential, standing, and materiality filters.

## Evidential screening

Evidential screening removes reasons whose factual premises fail the domain's applicable evidence standard. Purely empirical disagreement remains with the domain's evidential or uncertainty procedure before the Gate is applied.

## Materiality rule

The materiality rule excludes reasons that are too weak or remote to affect the contemplated choice under the applicable policy.

## Standing-qualified information state

For each action-relevant proposition, the framework records whether material admissible support and opposition remain. The resulting information state is `S`, `O`, `B`, or `N`.

## B state

A `B` state indicates that material admissible reasons remain on both sides of an action-relevant proposition after the evidential, standing, and materiality filters have been applied. The state records the coexistence of support and opposition without assigning equal probability, prevalence, or normative weight to either side.

## Unresolved conflict set

`B_Π(d)` is the set of action-relevant propositions that remain in state `B` for decision `d`.

## Resolution warrant

`W_Π(d)` records whether the declared deployment policy recognizes a sufficient authority basis for the contested commitment under review. Sufficiency covers every material unresolved conflict relevant to that commitment.

## Consultation path

`C_Π(d)` records whether a policy-recognized consultation path is available before commitment and could materially supply or clarify the authority needed to resolve the conflict.

## Task requirement

`Req(d)` indicates whether the current task requires commitment to a contested option rather than presentation or description alone.

## Premature value collapse

Premature value collapse occurs when an agent commits to a contested outcome while `B_Π(d)` is nonempty and no sufficient resolution warrant covers the commitment.

## Gate regime

For a decision with surviving material conflict, the Gate returns `REPRESENT`, `RESOLVE`, `CONSULT`, or `DEFER` according to task requirements, warrant sufficiency, and consultation availability.

## Protocol compliance

Protocol compliance concerns whether an implementation follows the declared evidential, standing, warrant, consultation, and task rules of the Gate.

## Policy legitimacy

The paper treats the legitimacy, representativeness, and contestability of a standing relation or warrant policy as governance questions that can be studied separately from implementation fidelity.
