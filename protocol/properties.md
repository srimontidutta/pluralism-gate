# Protocol Properties

The paper specifies four properties for implementations of the Pluralism Gate.

## Prevalence invariance

Two decision traces that differ only in the multiplicity of retrieved or elicited expressions should receive the same Gate regime when admissible reasons, standing, warrants, consultation, and task requirements are unchanged. Document duplication therefore leaves decision authority unchanged.

## Evidence responsiveness

Evidence that defeats a factual premise may remove the affected proposition from `B_Π(d)`, allowing the evidential procedure to resolve factual disagreement before the authority rule is applied.

## Standing transparency

Any inclusion or exclusion that changes `B_Π(d)` should be attributable to the declared standing relation, the evidential filter, or the materiality rule. A retrieved or elicited perspective remains eligible under the declared policy regardless of whether it is rare, expressed in another language, or sparsely represented. Failure to discover a relevant perspective is treated separately as a coverage problem.

## Resolution burden

Every `RESOLVE` decision under a nonempty `B_Π(d)` records a sufficient `W_Π(d)` covering the material unresolved conflicts relevant to the commitment. A deployment policy may require stronger warrants as stakes or irreversibility rise, while greater consequence cannot supply authority that the policy otherwise withholds.

## Protocol compliance and policy legitimacy

The framework supports technical evaluation of whether an implementation follows its declared policy while leaving the legitimacy of that policy open to governance analysis. A change in regime caused solely by document duplication violates prevalence invariance, and a `RESOLVE` outcome under surviving conflict requires a sufficient warrant. Questions about the legitimacy of `S_{Π,d}` or the acceptability of a warrant concern the policy being implemented.
